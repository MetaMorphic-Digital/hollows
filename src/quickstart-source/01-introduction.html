The Sins of Grisham Priory

Introduction

In wretched dimensional backwaters, vicious creatures stalk the blasted landscape and spread sickness, blight and madness through the wider world.

Born of torment and trauma, these Entities are a sickness and a stain on the land.

You, scarred with your own darkness, hunt these monsters.

Hollows is a tactical combat roleplaying game about slaying other people’s personal demons with malevolent weapons and revelling in the cursed joy of the Hunt.

This Quickstart is intended to give you a taste of the full game by pitching you headlong into a Hollow – a decrepit maze of greed and hunger known as Grisham Priory. Here, you will explore a desolate garden temple complex, meet the broken and dangerous people trapped inside, and destroy the monstrous Entities that rule this rotten place.

How To Play

Firstly, you’ll need to get some friends together. Most of you will be playing Hunters; one of you will take on the role of the Gamesmaster (or GM), who’s in charge of running the game and steering the story. If you’re reading this, odds are that you’ll be the Gamesmaster – that’s how these things normally shake out.

As part of the core Hollows crowdfunding campaign, we will be creating a custom set of tokens which you’ll be able to buy alongside the core book. If you’d like to create your own set of tokens, here’s what you’ll need, with some notes on how we made this work for playtesting:

  • A printout of the tactical grid. We recommend making this at A3 (or tabloid/ledger size, in the US).
  • A player token for each player. We used: 28mm minis, chess pieces, meeples.
  • About 20 Threat tokens. We used: glass drops, wooden cubes.
  • Two different kinds of terrain token, at least 4 of each. We used: poker chips.
  • At least one Curse token per player. We used: 6-sided dice.
  • An Entity token. We used: chess pieces, crystals.
  • A Bleeding token for the Knife. We used: coin
  • Two Ready tokens for the Armour. We used: coin
  • A Loaded token for the Shotgun. We used: coin
  • One Focus token per player, plus an extra four for the Rifle. We used: pennies, paperclips.
  • Resolve and Wound tokens for each player including the GM. We used: tiddlywinks, spin-down D20s.
  • Two D20s per player, or a pool you can all pick from to roll.

If you’re meeting up in person to play this game, you’ll need to copy, print or be prepared to tear out several pages of this document.

  • The character sheets (page 47)
  • The token sheet (page 59)
  • The tactical grid (page 59)

If you want to get old-fashioned, you can glue the token sheet to a piece of card and cut out the tokens. Otherwise, you can use the paper, or find stand-ins from things you already own. Things like coins, dice, poker chips, toy soldiers and spare infant teeth all work fine, as long as you’re consistent with what each type of item represents.

Each player will also need a pen or pencil and two twenty-sided dice (also known as D20s); if you don’t have access to dice you can find plenty of dice rolling applications online for free.

If you’re playing online, there is a downloadable asset pack available which includes form-fillable versions of the character sheets, and a large tactical grid image that you can use with your play tool of choice. At the time of writing, we are working on a Tabletop Simulator integration to help make play streamlined and seamless, as well as other VTT integrations. The latest info on these will always be available at rrdgames.com/hollows-resources.

Safety Tools

Hollows is a grim and unpleasant game, and it deals with themes of trauma, violence, death and abuse. All the players at the table have a responsibility to make the game fun and safe for everyone else. To make this as easy as possible, we recommend that you use safety tools that work for your table. We’ve listed some here that we use ourselves.

You shouldn’t feel you need to use all of these, and if there are other tools your group prefers, go with what works well for you. The most important thing is that when you sit down together to play, you agree to play safely and look out for each other, and provide some structure to support that socially. No detail or event in the fiction of the game is more important than a player’s comfort and safety.

Lines and Veils

A Line is a concept or event that someone doesn’t want to happen at all during the course of the game – you draw a line in front of it that cannot be overstepped. A Veil is a concept or event that someone doesn’t want the group to talk about in detail – it’s okay if it happens, but you draw a veil over it to put it at a distance.

Before the game starts, the GM should ask the table if they want to establish any Lines or Veils. Some people have none and some people have lots and they should both be welcome at the table. Respect the wishes of people at the table and change the fine details of the game to accommodate them.

Lines and Veils were first established by Ron Edwards.

The X Card

The X Card works as a safe-word for roleplaying games, allowing all participants to veto things they are not comfortable with. To use it, draw an X on a card and place it in the centre of the table. If anything happens during the game that makes someone feel uncomfortable in a way they don’t like (as opposed to uncomfortable in a way they do like – this is a horror game, after all) then they can lean forward, tap the card, and ask for it to be changed to something else. The person who tapped the card doesn’t need to explain why; just change it and move on.

If you’re playing online, we suggest using a text channel to type an X rather than a physical card. You might also want to agree a verbal trigger or a gesture as additional X options.

The X-Card was first established by John Stavropoulos.

Traffic Lights

You can also use the traffic light system, adapted from the BDSM community, which has the following three calls to make opting in and out of experiences very easy:

  • Red: Call “red” when you want the game to stop and change what’s going on.
  • Amber: Call “amber” when you want to pull back on whatever’s happening – make it less intense, less detailed, or just draw it to a close and move on.
  • Green: Call “green” when you’re enjoying what’s happening and want more of it.

Some Things You Can't Remove

If a player establishes something like “giant monsters”, “tactical combat”, or “thinly-veiled analogues of Victorian Britain” as Lines you might have a hard time running Hollows as intended. Most Lines and Veils can be incorporated into the game without much incident, but if the core elements of the game upset a player, they might be better off playing a different one. Talk to each other and work out what’s best for everyone involved.

The World of Hollows

The Isles

The Isles – the Kingdom, the Domain, the Royal Motherland – is where the Hunters live. The Isles are a once-powerful group of countries allied together under a bickering royal family and a parliament hungry for civil war. Struggling in the death throes of their empire, citizens sift through the industrial ruins and overgrown wilderness of their once-great country in search of meaning.

Malignancies

In shadowed spaces beyond the real, vast and wretched things pick at the stitches of the world in search of sustenance. These creatures – neither god nor devil, barely conscious, a roiling fever dream of want made manifest – are Malignancies, and they control the Isles through a thousand dark and wicked promises of power.

Hollows

If enough torment and hideousness are laid upon a person, a Malignancy plants the seed of a Hollow within them, and that seed will one day grow and burst into a cursed otherworld. Within the Hollow’s boundaries, the world is twisted into violent and unsettling shapes.

Hollows lie adjacent to the real world and are invisible to all but the most sin-stained and devastated, but the existence of a Hollow bleeds through the veil and causes sickness, madness, and death.

Entities

While each Hollow is home to a host of inhabitants, the most dangerous creatures within are Entities: great beasts that carry the weight of spite, grief and shame upon them and visit campaigns of violence and death upon their neighbours.

Some are mechanical monsters made of screeching metal and uncanny clockwork; others are flesh and blood broken and remade into strange forms; others still are semi-spectral, or garishly beautiful, or even a mob of individuals acting with a single grim intent. It is these Entities that hold the majority of the magical essence within a Hollow, and usually most of the authority as well.

Lords

Each Hollow centres on a particularly powerful Entity: a Lord, the origin of the trauma that grew cancerous and spread between realities. They are guarded – and sometimes challenged – by Vassals: lesser Entities within the Hollow, but still formidable beasts.

The only sure-fire means of closing a Hollow is to kill the Lord, severing the link that sustains the environment – but all the guns and soldiers in the Isles are worthless against a Lord and their Vassals, for normal people cannot enter the Hollow to bring them to bear.

Hunters

Hunters are not normal people.

Hunters carry the ungerminated seed of a Hollow within their souls; with some arcane tricks and metaphysical guesswork, they can kick in the back doors of these personal hells and walk in like they own the place.

Refusing to give in to the Malignancy makes them tougher, faster, and more powerful. Their injuries heal in hours, not months. By anointing themselves with the blood of fallen Entities they can nourish the seed and become ever-stronger, and ever more damned.

Hunters carry Weapons. Uncanny instruments that bruise reality with their very existence. Blades that beg to break through joint and sinew, guns that buck against their holsters, bloodstained bludgeons cracking bone and stone alike. Tools of terror that can rend an Entity asunder. The Weapons whisper: you are great. You are terrible. You are righteous. You are enough.

Hunters cannot die – not like regular folk are allowed to. If they are killed in battle, they awaken soon after, unharmed. Each death empowers the seed within them, and every Hunter knows that eventually that blight will blossom and they will become Lord of their personal Hollow. The longer it takes, the worse the Hollow will be.

You are a Hunter.

How Hollows Works

Much of Hollows takes place as an unstructured conversation between the players and the Gamesmaster, but when it’s exciting to introduce a random element, players roll twenty-sided dice (D20s) to determine the outcome of their actions.

Rolling Dice

When you roll a D20 to resolve an action, you will compare the result to two numbers – the stat you’re using to make the roll (page 7) and the Target Number (TN). If an action doesn’t state which stat to use, determine the correct one with the GM. The GM will know, or make up, the Target Number. In combat, the TN is the same as the relevant Defence stat of the Entity the Hunters are currently fighting. When exploring, the TN is usually defined by the Hazard or Thrall the Hunters are trying to get past.

Critical Failure

This mechanic, and the steps of success, are the same regardless of whether you are exploring, fighting, defending yourself, or doing any other action. The stakes will be different in different situations; if the mechanical details aren’t specified in the rules, work with the GM to apply the results to your situation.

If you are told to roll with advantage, roll two D20s and choose whichever result you prefer to resolve the action. If you are told to roll with disadvantage, roll two D20s and the GM can choose either result. Advantage and disadvantage cancel each other out, and you should never roll more than 2D20 to resolve a single action.

Later in this book, we go into more detail on what each result means in specific situations – attacking (page 16 ), defending (page 18 ), exploration (page 24) and more. Not all actions have outcomes listed for every dice result – we encourage the Gamesmaster to use their judgement and create a fun experience for everyone present by making their own rulings in response to player actions.

Hollows is a hard game. Failure is part of play, so failure should be interesting – work together to decide how adversity drives the story forward.

Blackjack Rules

Rolling in Hollows works a little like the card game blackjack. You’re aiming to roll as close to your stat value as possible, rather than simply trying to roll especially high or especially low. This means that each roll has different target numbers, and it can be a bit tricky for new players to wrap their heads around at first. It can help if you explicitly say what you’re aiming for when you start the roll. Just to make things clear: if the TN on a roll is higher than your stat, you aren’t able to roll a Superior Success. (You can still get a Critical Success, though, if you’re lucky.)

Characters

There are five pre-generated characters to choose from in this Quickstart. You’re free to call your Hunter whatever you like or stick with the descriptive titles we’ve given them. Each character sheet breaks down as follows:

Description: Each Hunter has a brief paragraph describing their outlook, motivations and behaviour. Change this entirely if you want – it’s just a suggestion.

Play X If You Want To...: Three bullet points that summarise the Hunter’s role in combat – whether they’re a damage dealer or a support character, a precision shooter or a wild brawler, etc.

Background: The Hunter’s background offers details on who they were before they chose this life. The Isles is a big place, and Hunters arise from all sorts of origins. When you act in accordance with your background during the Exploration phase, roll with advantage.

Statistics: There are five statistics (hereafter called stats) that govern your performance in Hollows. No stat can be increased above 19. The human average is around 7 – but Hunters are more, and less, than human.

  • Strong. A measure of physical fitness and power.
  • Hard. How difficult your character is to hurt, and how intimidating they are.
  • Quick. Your speed, gracefulness and ability to get out of the way of attacks.
  • Sharp. Your character’s situational awareness, insight and accuracy.
  • Wise. How in-tune your character is with science, mysticism, and the ways of the Hollow.

Resolve and Wounds: In addition to their stats, a Hunter also has two main measures of health.

Resolve is a measure of stamina, determination and physical fitness; Wounds are a measure of how much punishment you can take before your body gives up and you die.

A Hunter or Entity with 0 Resolve is Broken. If a Broken character would suffer Resolve damage, they suffer Wound damage instead; successful attacks against them all use the attack’s listed Wound damage instead of its Resolve. An Entity with 0 Wounds is dead; a Hunter with 0 Wounds is dying or dead.

Weapons: Each Hunter carries two Weapons – terrible tools of destruction bound up tight with their malaise. Each Weapon has a profile which details how much damage it does, where it can attack from and what stat it uses to attack Entities (page 16).

Abilities: The rancid energy bubbling within a Hollow floods your Weapons with power and lets you do things you’d never have imagined. At the start of the game, pick one ability from the list given for each of your Weapons. Each time you kill an Entity, you gain access to another ability of your choosing.

Ability Combinations

The rules in a Weapon ability can be applied to either Weapon you wield, unless stated otherwise in their description. For example, a Sword ability that lets you inflict additional damage when you’re in the same area as other Hunters also takes effect when you make attacks with your other Weapon.

Finding interesting or optimal combinations of abilities is part of the fun of playing Hollows. Even if you change the Weapons you wield later on during a campaign, you keep any Baptised abilities you gained while using them.

Sawn-off Characters

We’ve streamlined the characters and rules for this Quickstart, so there are a few things absent from character sheets that are important parts of the full game. These include Corruption, Equipment and your Seed: the awful event that sent you down the path of the Hunter, which infects other Hollows to remind you of the worst day of your life forever. You can learn more about these, and about the Isles and the setting of Hollows, in the core book.
