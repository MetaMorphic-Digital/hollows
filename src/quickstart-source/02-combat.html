Core Rules

Combat

The Battle

Combat is the pinnacle of a Hunter’s existence: in a world of uncertainty and terror, here they can destroy what stands against them with unrelenting viciousness. Should they be successful, they can drink deep of the curdled and rotten magic in the soul of this place, cleanse the world around them, and grow ever more dangerous.

The Grid

The grid shows the position of Hunters relative to the Entity. The Entity never changes position on the grid: it’s always at the centre of the fight. Your attention is always focussed on the Entity, and your position is only important in terms of its location.

The grid is divided into three main sections: Close, Ranged and Support.

Close areas are next to the Entity. These are: Front, Flank Right, Flank Left and Rear.

Ranged areas are more distant but still close enough to fight. These are: Ranged Front, Ranged Right, and Ranged Left.

These combat areas are arranged so that left and right are from the player's perspective as they face the Entity.

The Support area is out of range of the Entity’s attacks. A Hunter in Support recovers Weapon Capacity and Resolve at the start of their turn, and gains access to the Recover and Heal manoeuvres (see page <?>).

Different Rules

Combat feels different at the table to Exploration (page <OV>). They use different rules and enable different kinds of play, though they do share some similarities. Both of them use your stats to resolve every action. Exploring the Hollow is more abstract and descriptive, and Combat is more tactical and challenging.

No Escape

You can’t flee a fight once you face the Entity head-on. The land outside of the lair darkens pitch-black, doors lock tight, stairwells vanish and yawning voids fill the spaces where escape routes once ran. The Hollow wants this violence. So did you.

Moving On The Grid

The Move manoeuvre indicates that a Hunter travels from one location to another under their own volition. Move the token representing the Hunter from their current area to any adjacent area of the player’s choosing.

The Shift keyword on an Entity ability with a Hunter target indicates that the Entity is moving the Hunter by physically hurling them, luring them in with mind tricks, or otherwise changing their position in space. If a Hunter is Shifted, move the token representing the Hunter from their current area to an area indicated by the ability that’s being used. (Threat (page 13) can also be shifted around the grid in the same way by some abilities.)

When a Hunter Moves or is Shifted, they discard all terrain tags. If they Move or Shift more than one area, they are considered to be passing through all intervening grid areas, for the purposes of any effects that trigger when areas are entered or left.

The Reposition keyword usually indicates that the Entity has moved, rather than the Hunter. Move the token representing the Hunter from their current area to any adjacent area of the GM’s choosing, or the area specified by the ability that’s being used. The specific rules for an action with the Reposition keyword supersede the need for the area to be adjacent. For example: a charge action might Reposition a Hunter from Front to Rear, even though those two areas aren’t adjacent, as the Entity runs past (or through) the Hunter.

When a Hunter is Repositioned, they retain all terrain tags. They do not pass through any intervening areas between their starting and final position.

If an ability keyword has instructions attached to it (Shift towards Front, Reposition away from the Entity, Shift counter-clockwise) then the GM should attempt to fulfil those instructions. If there are none, they can move the token to whatever adjacent area they desire. If the instructions cannot be fulfilled, the GM should adjudicate an outcome themselves.

No Diagonals
Adjacent areas are orthogonally connected, in that they share a side (not a corner). You can’t Move or Shift diagonally.

Lock-On Targeting

Getting your head around moving on the grid can be a little tricky at first. It can help if you think about it like a video game: the camera, or the grid, is locked on to the Entity, so they don’t move. Your Hunters can choose to Move in relation to where the Entity is, and when the Entity moves, that’s represented by Repositioning things on the board. Both Hunters and Entities can Shift things around, moving them in relation to everything in play.

Actions

There are four types of action.

  • Attacks usually involve making a damaging attack against the Entity, or defending against one.
  • Manoeuvres are usually support actions. For example, they might involve moving, recovery, using items, or taking cover.
  • Immediate actions are instant, and can include both damaging and supporting abilities. Immediate actions are almost always triggered by specific conditions, including turn order, grid location, the actions of the Entity or of the Hunters. If something states that you “make an immediate Reload", for instance, you may do so outside your normal turn order, and it does not cost a manoeuvre action.
  • Interrupts are special Entity abilities which may be used after each Hunter turn ends. They usually have activation costs that the GM must decide to pay in order to use them.

Each round, Hunters and Entities may make either one attack and one manoeuvre or two manoeuvres on their turn. Entities may pay a cost to make one Interrupt at the end of each Hunter's turn. There is no limit to the number of immediate actions that either may take, as long as their conditions are met. Dying Hunters can’t make attack or manoeuvre actions (see page 22).

Terrain

During a Hunt, Hunters can use the environment to shield themselves from harm. On a player’s turn, they can use the Take Cover manoeuvre to attempt to take advantage of terrain with a successful roll under the relevant stat. There is a limited number of terrain tags in each fight, and no more than one Hunter can use each terrain tag at once, though two Hunters can have the same type of tag. A Hunter can have multiple terrain Tags at once, but no more than one of each type.

When you use the Take Cover manoeuvre, choose a terrain tag from the ones available in the terrain pool and place it under your token or on your character sheet. From this point until the tag is discarded or destroyed, you benefit from its effects. Roll under the stat listed next to the terrain type you chose; on a success, you suffer no ill effects. On a failure, choose one:

  • Suffer 1 Resolve damage as you over-exert yourself; or
  • Place 1 Threat on your area as you draw attention to yourself; or
  • Return the terrain tag to the pool.

When you attempt to gain the same type of tag that a Hunter in your area already has, roll with advantage.

When you Move or are Shifted, discard all terrain tags. When you are Repositioned, retain all tags. You can voluntarily discard terrain as an immediate, free action on your turn unless you're fighting an Entity with specific rules text that says otherwise.

When a terrain tag is discarded, return it to the central pool. When a terrain tag is destroyed it is not returned to the central pool, and is instead removed from play.

Terrain types

Each Entity has the terrain pool for their Hunt listed as part of their characteristics. The default terrain pool is three Elevated and three Sheltered.

  • Elevated. Towers, gantries, monoliths and cliffs. Roll Strong to claim. All attacks are rolled with advantage.
  • Sheltered. Ruins, foliage, smoke and barricades. Roll Quick to claim. When the Entity inflicts damage on you, take 1 less damage.

Setup, Doom and Terrain Pool

The Hollow doesn’t stand still and wait for the Hunters to kill it.

At the start of a battle, the GM should check the Doom track. Each Entity has certain changes to stats and abilities which trigger at different Doom thresholds. Everything listed at or under the current Doom level takes effect for this fight. Doom is gained (and lost) by the Hunters during Exploration (see page 24).

The GM also builds the pool of terrain tags. The default terrain pool is three Elevated and three Sheltered tags, but each Entity description specifies the exact mix of terrain used. See page 11 for more on terrain.

Starting Locations and Turn Order

Turn brackets are defined at the start of a Hunt. Each player rolls Sharp with a TN equal to the Entity’s Ranged Defence.

  • On a standard success, they may choose to either place their token anywhere on the grid or act before the Entity.
  • On a superior success, they may do both.
  • On a critical success, they may do both and also take an additional manoeuvre on their first turn.
  • On a failure, they do neither: the GM places their token on the grid, and they must act after the Entity.

All players who do not choose to act before the Entity will act after it each round. Players may take their turns in any order within their bracket.

After successful players have placed their tokens, the GM places the remaining tokens on the grid and makes a note of the turn brackets. Any Hunter who chose to act before the Entity goes first, then the Entity, and then any remaining Hunters.

Turns and Actions

A turn is an individual character's opportunity to make an impact on the battlefield. Each Hunter and the Entity gets one turn before any of them may act again. All of these turns in order are a round. Once one round is complete, the next begins. Once turn brackets are decided at the start of combat (see Turn Order, above), they remain the same until the combat ends, but Hunters can act in any order within their brackets each round.

Each round, Hunters and Entities may make either one attack and one manoeuvre or two manoeuvres on their turn. Entities may pay a cost to make one Interrupt at the end of each Hunter’s turn. There is no limit to the number of immediate actions that either may take, as long as their conditions are met.

Dying Hunters can’t make attack or manoeuvre actions: see
page 22.

When the rules say that a character may perform an immediate action, it happens instantly – sometimes outside of the normal turn order. Immediate actions do not count towards a character’s maximum actions on their turn.

Allies and Targeting
You cannot be your own ally: abilities that target “an ally in your area” must be used on a different Hunter. If you have an ability that targets “a Hunter in your area”, you can use that on yourself.

Using Abilities

Both Hunters and Entities have access to a variety of abilities, including generic manoeuvres available to all, and other abilities that are unique to the Weapon wielded or the Entity being hunted.

There are a variety of ways that abilities can be triggered or activated. Some are conditional, some require trigger events or costs to be paid, and others may be used in place of your action or manoeuvre on your turn. In general, if you fulfil all the requirements to use one of your abilities, you may choose to use it but do not have to.

Entity abilities often also specify targets or target areas, like “all Hunters in Close” or “one Hunter in a Flank area”. There must be at least one target who matches the ability’s target description in order for it to be triggered.

Some ability effects are automatic, while others depend on a dice roll or on additional conditions. Generally the ability text will say what happens and when, and sometimes multiple abilities on both sides of the fight can trigger off each other. If there’s doubt about what order things should happen, the GM decides.

Threat

Threat is a measure of an Entity’s intent, attention and behaviour over the course of a Hunt. It is placed on the grid in specific areas, and can be placed, manipulated and spent by both Hunters and Entities in various ways. Successful Hunter attacks generate Threat, as do some abilities. The GM can spend Threat to enhance the Entity’s attacks and defence (page 19) as well as to trigger Interrupt actions. Usually, if a Hunter uses Threat, it will be related to a specific Weapon ability.

When using the combat grid, physically place Threat tokens on different areas of the grid to show how much Threat is present. These can be beads, dice or other tokens if you don’t have the Hollows token box. In this book, whenever the text refers to spending, removing, or manipulating Threat, this should be represented by movement of Threat tokens on the grid.

Ability
Stacking

Either you're affected by an ability or you're not, so unless otherwise stated, abilities don't stack. For example: the Got Your Back Sword ability grants +1 Resolve damage to allies in your area if that area contains Threat. Two Sword bearers with Got Your Back in the same area don’t grant +2.
For similar reasons, a Hunter cannot possess the same ability more than once. If it’s unclear whether effects can apply simultaneously, work it out with the GM: that’s what they’re there for.

Damaging and Healing

Resolve and Wounds

Resolve is a measure of a Hunter or Entity’s stamina, defensive capability, and spirit. It is easier to remove (and restore) than Wounds.

Wounds are a measure of a Hunter or Entity’s toughness, willpower and survivability. They are harder to remove and heal than Resolve, and most Entities do not have a way to heal them.

On attacks and other damaging abilities, Resolve and Wounds are listed as X/Y, with X being the Resolve damage inflicted and Y being the Wounds inflicted. Which type of damage you inflict or suffer will depend on your dice rolls, and whether or not the target of the ability is Broken.

Breaking and Being Broken

When a Hunter or an Entity’s Resolve is 0, they are Broken, and whenever an attack against them would inflict Resolve damage it now inflicts its listed Wound damage instead.

If an attack or other source of damage only lists Resolve damage, not Wounds, then it does no damage against a Broken target. If an ability calls for the user to “spend Resolve” as an activation cost, the user cannot activate it if they have no Resolve to spend.

Spending Health

If an ability asks you to “spend Resolve”, then you may choose to spend Resolve if you have it, in order to trigger that ability. You cannot trigger the ability if you have no Resolve. If an ability states that you “suffer Resolve damage” as a cost, however, then you take no damage if you have no Resolve left.

Hunter Turns

Hunter Attacks

A Hunter’s basic attack is an attempt to harm an Entity with a Weapon. Each Weapon form has its own core attack, and Hunters may use whichever they prefer. A Weapon’s profile dictates what areas it can be used in and what stat is used to determine the attack’s success.

Weapons also grant access to various abilities, some of which augment their basic attack, or provide new ways to attack an Entity. These are described and rolled in the same way.

As an immediate action in response to being attacked, the Entity may spend Threat in the attacker’s area to increase its relevant Defence by 1 for each Threat spent until the end of the attacker’s turn. This must be spent after the attack is declared but before the dice are rolled.

When you attack, make a roll as described in the basic rules (page 6). You are aiming to roll under your attack stat, but over the Entity’s relevant Defence. Resolve the roll using the table below. If you are rolling with advantage, roll an additional D20 and choose either to resolve the action. If you are rolling with disadvantage, roll an additional D20 and the GM may choose either dice. Advantage and disadvantage cancel each other out, and they do not stack: you never roll more than 2D20 on a single action.

If you would inflict Wound damage with your attack, you can choose to inflict Resolve damage instead. If you would inflict Resolve damage and the Entity has no Resolve remaining – it is Broken – inflict the Wound damage value of your Weapon instead.

Hunter Manoeuvres

You have access to five core manoeuvres. You can usually perform one on your turn; if you rolled a critical success on your setup roll for the Hunt, you can also perform an additional manoeuvre on your first turn. You can also swap your action each turn for a manoeuvre.

  • Focus : Gain Focus (page 17), to a maximum of 1.
  • Guard: Restore 2 Resolve, or attempt to revive a dying Hunter in your area.
  • Move: Travel between two adjacent grid areas (see page 10).
  • Reload: Regain 1 Capacity on a Weapon you are holding, up to its maximum.
  • Take Cover : Claim a terrain tag (see page 11).
  • Use: Activate an ability, item or power as defined in its description.

Support Area And Support Manoeuvres

When you start your turn in the Support area, you may restore your Weapons’ Capacity (if they have it) to their maximum values, and restore 2 Resolve. Hunters in the Support location also have access to additional manoeuvres, listed below:

  • Recover : Roll Hard. On any success, restore 4 Resolve or 2 Wounds (your choice). On a failure, restore 2 Resolve.
  • Heal : Roll Wise. On a success, restore 2 Wounds to another Hunter in Support or restore 1 Wound to yourself. On a failure, restore 1 Wound to another Hunter in Support.

Focus

Taking time to steady the mind can pay dividends. As an immediate action, characters can spend Focus to roll with advantage on their next roll. In general, a Hunter cannot have more than 1 Focus – either you have it or you don’t. (Unless that Hunter is carrying a Rifle, which means they can store up to 5 Focus at any time, or has some other way to bend this rule.) Hunters always start the Hunt with 0 Focus.

Interrupts and the End of the Turn

At the end of a Hunter’s turn, the Entity has an opportunity to use an Interrupt from its character sheet. Once per Hunter turn, the GM may choose to pay any cost associated with an Interrupt in order to use it. This can take place multiple times per round, after every Hunter turn, if the GM so decides and can pay the cost.

Entity Turns

An Entity may make one attack and one manoeuvre on its turn, just like a Hunter can. Also like Hunters, the GM may choose instead to take two manoeuvres. They can't use Interrupts on their turn – only during the Interrupt phase at the end of Hunter turns.

The GM never rolls dice: Hunters roll to defend against their attacks.

Each attack on an Entity’s sheet has rules text which states its target, difficulty, the stat the Hunter uses to avoid the attack, damage dealt, and any special conditions or effects.

Do Some Damage

If the GM spends Threat to boost an attack, it’s more likely to cause damage. Wound damage is much harder to heal than Resolve damage, but taking the players down to zero Resolve means that every hit is a Wound. Use this to pressure the Hunters and make fights more deadly.

Entity Manoeuvres

Each Entity has its own set of manoeuvres, and they all have access to the following three as a default:

  • Prowl. Target one area and one adjacent area. All Hunters in the first area test Quick. On a failure, they are Repositioned to the adjacent area.
  • Shrug Off.Restore 3 Resolve.
  • Turn Around. All Hunters in Close are Repositioned clockwise or counter-clockwise – choose one and apply it to all targets.

Placing and Spending Threat

Threat is a measure of an Entity’s intent, attention and behaviour over the course of a Hunt. There are three main ways Threat can be added to the grid:

  • When a Hunter hits with an attack, the GM places Threat on their area in response.
  • When a Hunter uses an ability that specifies Threat is placed on an area.
  • When the Entity’s turn ends, the GM places Threat equal to the Entity’s Threat Per Round.

After the Entity has made both of their actions, at the end of their turn, the GM first checks whether the current Threat on the grid is lower or higher than the Entity’s Threat Cap. If it is lower, the GM may place Threat equal to the Entity’s Threat Per Round on the grid, spread across any grid areas except Support, up to the Threat Cap total. If the Threat Cap has been reached, the GM must remove any Threat over the Cap from the grid. They must not place additional Threat, but may instead Shift the same number of Threat that they would have placed if the Cap had not been reached.

Curse

Some Entities can corrupt, mark or otherwise influence the environment through Curse. When an attack says it inflicts Curse, place the amount of Curse specified on the Hunter’s character sheet, using a 6-sided counter or a stack of tokens. A Hunter, Entity or area cannot have more than 6 Curse placed on it at any one time. If additional Curse would be placed, it is instead discarded.

Curse tokens can only be removed by the means listed in the Entity’s description or abilities, and during some fights, it may not be possible to remove Curse. At the end of a battle, win or lose, clear all Curse tokens from the grid, Hunters and the Entity.

Entity Actions Outside Their Turn

Unlike most Hunters, Entities have opportunities to act when it isn’t their turn. Each Hunter turn ends with an opportunity for an Interrupt: an Entity ability which usually carries a cost to activate or trigger. The GM may choose to spend the cost of an Interrupt at the end of any Hunter’s turn; there is no technical restriction on how many times per round an Interrupt may trigger, but it may only trigger once per Hunter turn. Interrupts do not trigger on the Entity’s turn.

In addition, most Entities have abilities which trigger when they are Broken, either for the first time or every time in a Hunt. These are immediate actions which take place after the attack which reduces the Entity to zero Resolve. If an Entity is Broken, it cannot spend Resolve as a cost to trigger abilities.

Pull No Punches

GMs: it is your job to be fans of the players. That is, until it comes to combat. When the battle begins, you need to step up and be the schoolyard bully. Do not pull your punches, and certainly do not let pity cloud your judgement. If you see a healer isolated and injured, then you should absolutely take advantage of that. The players must learn how to protect them in fights where the odds are massively stacked against them. They have all the tools they need to survive and triumph; death is not the end, and defeat is an integral part of learning and progressing in Hollows (especially in the full game). Be vicious, wicked, and cruel in your portrayal of the Entities, and the players will learn to be better in each and every fight. Our aim when designing these creatures is that in any given battle the Entity will kill at least two Hunters; in Grisham Priory we have been a little kinder with the early fights, but difficulty deliberately ramps up as you play through the quickstart. A perfect fight would leave one Hunter with exactly 1 Wound left standing. Do not worry about going too far; you can always change the difficulty of the fight later. Harder is always better.

Curse Varieties

The Tailor Entity in this book uses Curse to punch crimson threads through the Hunters’ bodies, so the Curse tokens go on their character sheets. Other Entities in the Hollows core game can place it on the grid to affect the battlefield, or on themselves to gain power.

Example of Play

Max is playing the Experiment, who is fighting the Guardian Hound along with the Priest, played by Sam. At the start of combat, they both roll for turn order.

Sam rolls against their Sharp of 14 and the Hound’s Ranged Defence of 12. They get a 13: higher than the TN but under their stat. This is a superior success, allowing them to place their token on the grid and act before the Entity.

Max rolls against their Sharp of 11 and the Hound’s Ranged Defence of 12: because the TN is higher than their stat, they can’t roll a superior success but could still roll a critical. They get a 15: a failure, which means they act after the Entity and the GM places their token.

The GM places the Agent token in Front, while Sam decides to place the Priest token in Flank Left. Sam takes their turn first.

“I’m going to try to flank the Hound, and get on its bad side. I’ll use my Shotgun’s core attack first: that runs off Sharp.”

Sam flips their Loaded token to Unloaded to make the attack. They roll a 6, which is below their Sharp of 14, and also below the Hound’s Close Defence of 9: a standard success, inflicting 2 Resolve damage. The GM places a Threat token on their area.

“OK, so my bullet grazes the dog’s shoulder, forcing it to dodge back a little. I see I’ve gotten its attention – I’ll Move backwards into Ranged Left to get away.”

The Hound can’t make its Interrupt attack this turn, because Sam has moved away from the Threat. Now it’s the Entity’s turn.

“Right… the dog is still fixated on the Priest, so it’s going to Prowl towards you. That will Reposition the Priest back to where they were, into Flank Left. Roll Quick to see if it's faster than you.”

The Priest only has a Quick of 7, and Sam rolls a 14; the Priest is Repositioned back where they came from.

It's bigger than you, and faster, so it chases after you and snarls. Then it bares its teeth and lunges for you, attempting to Grab and Shake. Roll against your Hard to defend.”

The Priest has a Hard of 10, and the attack also has a TN of 10. Sam rolls a 3: a standard success below both numbers, avoiding Wound damage but suffering Resolve.

“The Hound’s sudden advance alarms you: you only just manage to dodge away from its vicious, dagger-like teeth in time! You take 3 Resolve damage. And that’s the end of my turn, so the Hound places Threat – 4 in total.”

The GM places 2 Threat on Front, and 2 on Flank Left – splitting the Threat between the two Hunters – and motions to Max to take their turn.

“I’ve seen the Hound pounce on my friend so I’m not taking any chances. I’ve got a Pistol with the Skirmisher ability, so first I’m going to Move into Rear – that costs my manoeuvre for the turn, right?

The GM says:

“Right! You circle the Hound, gun in one hand and dagger in the other, looking for an opening. You’re moving to a Close area, so you get +2 to your Quick. What’s next?”

Max checks their character sheet, and says:

“OK, so because I’m in Rear, I get extra damage from Backstab – that’s +1/+1. And I’m rolling my Quick to attack with the Knife. I guess I’m sneaking up behind it while it’s distracted and trying to slash at the tendons in its back legs, aiming to do some serious damage.”

The GM nods, and says:

“So you’re aiming to roll under your Quick, which is now 16, and ideally over the Hound’s Close Defence of 9. Let’s roll and see what happens.”

Max rolls a 12: a superior success, inflicting 1 Wound damage plus 1 more from their Backstab ability.

“Hey, that means it’s Bleeding!”

The GM takes the Bleeding token and flips it to show that the Entity is now taking damage each time Max’s turn starts. In response to the damaging attack, the GM places 2 Threat on Rear, where the Experiment is standing, and says:

“You take advantage of the Hound’s distraction to sneak beneath its haunches and slash at its exposed feet in a single, whip-quick gesture, exposing the bone and tendon beneath its skin. The Hound howls in agony as blood spurts from its injuries, and lunges towards you. It’s going to Snap and Snarl.”

The GM spends 1 Threat from Rear to trigger the Interrupt.

“Make a defence roll, TN8 against your Strong.”

Max rolls a 9.

“Unlucky! The Hound manages to catch your firing arm, grazing your flesh with its jagged teeth. It draws blood: take 1 Wound. And that’s the end of our first turn. Sam, you’re up again."

Death and Dying

When an Entity has 0 wounds remaining, it dies and the Hunters can harvest it for grim power, through a Baptism.

When a Hunter has 0 Wounds remaining, they are dying. Set their Resolve to 0, if it's not already. Dying Hunters can’t make attack or manoeuvre actions. At the start of each of their turns, a dying Hunter must roll under their Hard stat – if they fail, they die, and are removed from the tactical grid. If they succeed, they cling to life for a while longer. On a critical success, they are revived with 1 Resolve and 1 Wound.

If a dying Hunter suffers any additional Wound damage, they die. They roll to defend as normal against Entity attacks. A dying Hunter cannot be the target of Interrupt actions from the Entity; they’re not worth it.

Any other Hunter can attempt to revive a dying Hunter by performing a Guard manoeuvre in their area. Doing this restores 1 Resolve and heals 1 Wound to the dying Hunter (instead of the standard effect), and the revived Hunter can make actions as normal on their turn.

A Hunter can only be revived once per Hunt; the second time they’re reduced to 0 Wounds, they immediately die.

Hunters don’t get to stay dead. After dying in Grisham Priory, they awaken in the Sanctum (page 34) with a fierce metaphysical headache and a grudge. In the core Hollows game, they awaken in their Refuge: a base of operations which they use as a home outside of space and time, and which may be upgraded to contain many useful and interesting things.

Hunters cannot return back to the fight under their own steam: they must pass through a door back into the Hollow. The Hollow actively resists the creation of any such doors until the Hunt is over, whether successful or otherwise, and opening the door increases Doom by 3. However, dead Hunters remain linked to the outcome of the Hunt: if the Entity is killed, any Hunters who died during the fight gain the benefits of the Baptism.

Baptism

When you slay an Entity you anoint yourself with its blood, draining energy out of the Hollow and into your body. Broken bones snap and set, torn wounds close and blood runs back into the rift, and shattered teeth settle back into their sockets.

All surviving Hunters who killed an Entity restore all Resolve and Wounds to their maximum values. Doom increases by 1, as the Hollow shudders and recognises the danger the Hunters pose.

What’s more, the Seed within them pulses and roars in response to fresh blood. Taking down an Entity results in a burst of malignant power that takes up residence in the Hunters’ Weapons, whether they are standing over its corpse or recovering in the Refuge. Killing a Vassal Entity gives the Hunters access to one of the Weapon abilities listed on their character sheet, for the rest of the Hollow they are currently in. Killing a Lord Entity makes one of those advances permanent, and closes the Hollow, curing the world around it.

An Insidious Decay

In the full version of Hollows, death is more complicated. The more often you die, the more likely it is you will blossom into a Hollow yourself.

Wretched Investments

When you kill a Lord, choose one of the Weapon abilities you earned during the Hollow and make it permanent. The others are removed from your character as the parasitic reality is sutured shut. As this Quickstart deals only with a single Hollow, you won’t need to worry about this – but it’s worth knowing that this one of the main ways a Hunter grows in power.

Foregone Conclusions

You might get to a point in a fight where it seems as though the players can’t possibly win, and it’s no fun to continue slogging through it for either side. If this happens, surviving players can elect to skip to the end and simply narrate their gruesome deaths; they all awaken in the Sanctum as normal.

Reviving

During The Sins of Grisham Priory , the Hunters have access to the Sanctum: a special space outside the Hollow itself, but closely tied to it, and standing in for the full Refuge that exists in the core game. It's populated by Varrick, a trapped and isolated Hunter who's summoned the players to help them get free.

When the Hunters are revived in the Sanctum, Varrick is there to act as a helpful guide (or goad) to push them onwards. Have them offer the Hunters cups of tea, sympathy, support, critique, or anything you feel they would enjoy; these can be fun scenes between gruesome battles and a nice opportunity for character roleplay. Varrick flatly refuses to come with them or offer combat support: they are far too scared of what they might become if they die again, and they’ve lost too many friends and colleagues to the Corruption that sits at the heart of every Hunter.

When it’s time for the Hunters to get back in the fight, they use Varrick’s Key to open the door back into the Priory (costing them 3 Doom in the process). They can be dumped right back at the start of the fight: as soon as they leave the safety of the Sanctum, the Hunt begins again.

Evolving Tactics

If all the player characters die during a Hunt, you can offer them the chance to swap out some of their abilities and modify their tactics. They may replace any one ability with a different one from the same Weapon by using Varrick’s rudimentary forge to modify their kit, scavenging materials from the Hollow, or communing directly with the malevolent intelligence caught within the steel.

Not the Only Way

It’s not without cost or risk, but: Hunters can Anchor a Lord’s spirit in place and save them from utter destruction. You can learn more about Anchoring in the core Hollows rulebook.
