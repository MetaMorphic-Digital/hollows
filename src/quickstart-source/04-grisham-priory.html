Grisham Priory

Content Warnings

Dogs, animal attacks, dragons, spider analogues, poverty, starvation, cruelty to animals, needles, religion and religious violence.

The Tale

Bishop Damien Loxley oversaw the diocese of Grisham, and was renowned for his keen tithe-taking and enforced piety. While acts of service and tending the sacred orchards were all well and good, he knew how the world worked – money, and lots of it. His desire to uplift his charges from poverty through the blessings of the land became an all-consuming obsession with wringing each and every spare penny from those who lived in, or even passed through, his territory.

Grisham flourished, and Bishop Loxley flourished alongside it. His robes were more splendid than any other clergyman at court, his festivals and feasts more lavish,
his gardens overflowing with rose and orchid. He did well, and those under his care starved. For decades he was able to shield himself from the harm he was causing, but it couldn’t last forever. One fateful afternoon, when the malnourished peasantry of his parish broke into his priory to find supplies, he lashed out in fury. A dozen men died, and a dozen families were turned out of their hovels for daring to stand against him.

Something blossomed in Loxley that day. A whisper from the dark between worlds took root in his soul – a revelation. Not only did he deserve the riches he had taken, but it was right that he had them. The land had offered up this bounty in exchange for his faith and hard work. Those who would seek to take them are worse than thieves – heretics.

Personal Hells, Made To Order

Each Hollow has a Tale – the genesis of the seed planted in the Lord by one of the Grand Malignancies that circle the Isles like carrion crows. As with the Roots that come later on in each individual zone, these are designed to give the GM some background on the events that lead to the hollowing and help them roleplay and invent details on the fly.

Strange Things

Each Hollow is accompanied by a collection of Strange Things that can be useful when describing the Hollow to your players. They often foreshadow future Entities or locations, and are provided to aid the GM in adding colour and flair to their descriptions of the Hollow.

  • A coin bounces and rolls through a crack in the floor.
  • Webs of fine red thread form trip-wires across thoroughfares.
  • Porcelain fungi sprout from the desiccated earth.
  • The wall is worn thin, revealing stained glass portraits beneath the stone.
  • A thrall begs for food that they cannot draw nourishment from.
  • A thin, greasy flurry of rain spatters against a window or roof.
  • A song of praise to the Bishop echoes down a corridor.
  • Your Weapon refuses to draw blood unless it’s “paid”.
  • A bloated pig, dressed with ribbons and prayers,
    farts and snorts its way through the Hollow in search of food.
  • The ceiling collapses after decades of disrepair.

Priory Background

Sometimes while roleplaying, you might want to introduce additional encounters or rewards for your players. Here are some incidental items and hazards that your players might find while exploring, in addition to (or instead of) the relics that are associated with specific areas and inhabitants of the Hollow. Bear in mind that introducing new Hazards or Relics can change the game balance in significant ways; consider introducing them if your players are having a hard (or too easy) time.

Pacing Yourselves

In the core Hollows game, most Hollows will have a Doom track that goes up to 25. In this quickstart, we’ve provided a shorter track and a more immediate first combat, to get you into the action more quickly.

The Hunters will accrue Doom through exploration, so the longer they take to explore the more risky their eventual battles will become. But on the other hand, they’ll know more about the Lord and its Vassals, and maybe even have access to valuable tools they can use to change the fight. It’s up to you as a group to decide how many risks to take in discovering the story of this place.

Entering the Hollow

There are a hundred ways and more to enter a Hollow. Some Hunters rely on arcane tools like black scrying mirrors, rune-etched pendulums, or ritual candles; some weaken the barrier between worlds with sacred earth, rowan roots and devil’s-salt then force their way through; some converse with the lost spirit of the Hollowed, or pose as a loved one, and con their way inside.

This time, you were pulled in. As you prepared to make your entrance, the world stuttered and cracked around you, and reality seared with burning sigils and fell away.

The Hollow takes the form of an overgrown church complex; you appear in front of a great door that seals it off from storm-blasted wastes that stretch away in every direction.

You are in…

1. The Graveyard

Root: The Bishop sacrificed a lot of people to get to his lofty position. He never thinks of them at all.

A dilapidated burial ground. Maybe two hundred graves lie here, with ornamentation ranging from simple wooden effigies of pig skulls to great trees carved from marble. Every single one of the graves is anonymous. Here and there, the Hound has dug up a coffin or two and eaten the remains, but it looks like very few people have been buried here in recent memory.

Before you stands a wide-eyed, haggard-looking occultist clutching a staff in both their hands. The magic circle they scratched into the dirt is already blowing away.

“It worked!” they exclaim.

“First things first: take this key.” They thrust a black iron key into your hand with shaking, mud-stained fingers.

“It works on any door,”they continue. “If anything goes wrong, you’ll be safe there.” They pause for a second, exhale, and collect themselves. A relieved smile warms their face.

“Where are my manners? My apologies, you can call me –”

The doors behind slam open and a hound the size of a carriage lands on them and tears them apart in seconds. It snarls and drags what remains of their broken body back through the doorway.

Welcome to the Hollow.

The First Combat

The Quickstart begins with a battle against the Guardian Hound (page 38), a vassal Entity. Show the players the tactical grid and have them roll for placement and turn order (page 12) against the Hound’s Ranged Defence of 12.

First, the group of Hunters who act before the Entity take their turns (page 16). After every Hunter turn ends, you may (and should!) make an Interrupt action if you are able to spend Threat to do so.

Then, you take the Entity’s turn (page 18). At the end of your turn, place Threat equal to the Entity’s Threat Per Turn (page 19).

After that, the remaining Hunters take their turns. You may make Interrupt actions after these turns as well.

Once all the Hunters have acted, the first round is over and a new round begins. Continue until the Guardian Hound is killed and perform your first Baptism (page 22). In the unlikely event all the Hunters die, they awaken in the Sanctum (page 34).

Introductions

The first round of combat is a great time to get your players to introduce their characters. If you like, ask each one to describe their physical appearance as they square up against the Entity, and tell the group a little about how their Weapons look and behave as they use them. Big showy cinematic descriptions of combat aren’t to everyone’s taste, but they can really enhance the way the game feels to play, and help your players feel ownership over their new Hunters.

Baptism, Healing & Advancement

After the Hound is defeated, the players receive a Baptism: they are imbued with a small portion of the Entity’s power, which allows them to restore their Resolve to full, heal some Wounds, and advance. Each Hunter alive at the end of the battle restores their Resolve to its maximum value, then rolls Hard; on a success, they restore Wounds to maximum, and on a failure they restore Wounds to half their maximum value, rounding up.

Each Hunter involved in the Battle – whether living or dead at the end – may choose one new Weapon ability. Hunters advance in this way after every successful Battle.

Tracking Doom

Now the Hound is dead, you'll want to start tracking Doom. It starts at zero, and is increased when the players fail Exploration rolls or open doors into the Sanctum. As it increases, it makes combat more challenging. You might want to describe it as a sense of foreboding, or in terms of the supernatural strangeness of this place intensifying, using the Strange Things list on page 30 as a guide.

Entity Rewards

In general, Entities in Hollows don’t grant equipment as loot; instead, they grant Balm, Blood and Bone.

Balm restores all Hunters back up to their maximum Resolve and gives them an opportunity to heal Wounds.

Blood is a rush of power that floods into the Hunters and takes up residence in their Weapons, unlocking new abilities within them. Describe this however works for you: mystical energy, light or darkness, smoke, thick ichor, whisperings from the Weapons, or trinkets taken from the corpse and tied to the Hunters’ Weapons.

Bone represents physical tokens that carry a small part of the Entity’s power, ossified into solid form. In this Quickstart, there is no way to spend these resources, but if you’d like to continue the game beyond this scenario – or your players want some loot as a reward – describe the dog’s collar, a hank of its hair, or a chunk of its chain, thrumming with dark power and begging to be carried.

The Sanctum

Buried within the walls of the Priory is the Sanctum – the paradimensional foxhole that Varrick, the occultist who pulled the Hunters into the Hollow, is holed up inside. The Sanctum is a cramped, wood-panelled room with mould on the walls and flagstone flooring. Several pews have been dragged together to form a sort of makeshift bed against one wall.

The Hunters can insert the key that Varrick gave them into any door; doors that lack keyholes will morph one into being in response to the key’s proximity. The door now opens into the Sanctum instead of its original location. The creatures from the Priory cannot enter here.

If the Hunters are eager to discover more about the key after the Guardian Hound fight, encourage them to explore among the mausoleums of the Graveyard, or suggest that the key pulls on their fingers as they approach the door to the next area, the Cloisters.

When the Hunters enter, they restore their Resolve and Wounds to their starting values as they catch their breath and bandage their injuries.

After dying, a Hunter will wake up here with their Resolve and Wounds restored to their starting values.

Whenever a Hunter opens the door to the Sanctum, no matter how many Hunters pass through, add 3 Doom to reflect the time and supernatural disturbance involved.

Varrick is hiding out here, and they are terrified of leaving the Sanctum; although they too come back to life here if they die in the Hollow, they’re feeling increasingly unstable after a dozen deaths. The next one might be their last, and then they’d blossom into a Hollow all their very own.

Take Refuge

In the full Hollows game, players gain access to their own sanctum – a snicket in space and time, known as the Refuge. In time, they’ll be able to welcome in other lost souls, construct new rooms and arcane devices, augment and improve their abilities and stats, and even rescue the tormented souls of Lords to live in peace via Anchoring.

2. The Cloisters

Root: The Bishop syphoned resources from his diocese to furnish his estate with lavish trappings while the common folk under his care struggled to survive.

The people trapped here have erected a makeshift farm in the outdoor space between buildings, but it’s hard going. Tatty-looking potato and turnip plants poke through the thin and gritty soil in clumps. Furniture taken from inside the temple has been converted into shelters and barricades – smouldering devotional candelabras teeter precariously atop piles of wardrobes, pews and moth-eaten chaise longues. The entire thing’s a death-trap but they’ve held out until now.

At the centre of the cloister is a shrine covered in images of the dead, painstakingly drawn in charcoal by a careful, shaking hand. This shrine holds the Accusing Ashes; the congregation are unwilling to let them go, as they’re the last remnants of their deceased loved ones. Bargaining, threatening or bribing them could work – attacking them definitely will, if you’ve the stomach for it.

3. The Wormery

Root: The Bishop strived to maintain a public image of proper piety, building sacred wormeries, compost heaps and pig farms throughout the district. Anyone who displeased him would wind up as mulch in a matter of months.

A subterranean warren, dimly lit by candles and braziers. It houses a multitude of floor-to-ceiling interlinked glass boxes filled with decomposing vegetable matter, millions of worms and the occasional skeleton. A whirling mass of worshipers adorned with paper streamers sing the praises of the Bishop and his generosity; on occasion, they form themselves into the shape of a great wyrm with a lit torch at the head, and dance through the maze of glass and dirt.

Among the material that makes up their costumes, a bundle of paper stands out as important – these are the Dolorous Receipts, guarded by these zealots to keep them safe from the Tailor.

4. The Vestry

Root: The Tailor had a complicated upbringing and some controversial appetites that would see her cast out of polite society; the Bishop kept receipts of her indiscretions, and used them to blackmail her into his service.

Silks, bolts of linen and sheets of fine lace hang from the ceiling in this chamber, but they do little to absorb the noise of the chattering, grinding textile machinery that rattles away of its own accord. Great piles of dye reach as high as the rafters – saffron yellow, rich purple and boreal green-forest – and man-sized spindles of crimson thread blur with the speed of their ceaseless rotation.

At the centre of this web crouches The Tailor (page 40), who endlessly weaves a grand tapestry at the behest of the Bishop. Their vital work interrupted, they lash out at the Hunters as they approach.

Hanging Cloth

Bolts of material hang from the rafters, stiff with sharp needles from the Tailor’s ministrations.

Either: Sharp TN14, 1/1 Doom; or: Strong TN10, 2/2 Doom. Find or make a path through the hanging cloth to confront The Tailor.

Roll to Hunt

Often, the Hunters will come across an Entity battle which requires them to traverse a Hazard. Usually these Hazards carry the risk of increasing Doom. Only one Hunter needs to successfully bypass the Hazard; it’s assumed they show the others the safe way to pass. Once the group has traversed the Hazard, the fight begins automatically: they attract the Entity’s attention and violence cannot be avoided.

5. The Crypt

Root: The Bishop guarded his money, and his secrecy, with the utmost care.

A crumbling, damp limestone vault carved out of the rock beneath the Temple, with tree-roots questing down from the surface above. The central chamber yawns impossibly tall above the Hunters’ heads; around the entrance, a ward of withered rowan roots has been placed to offer protection. As the Hunters approach they can hear devotional chanting issuing forth from cracked throats.

Lining the walls of the structure and enshrined in mausolea are dozens of sarcophagi decorated with statues of the Bishop in repose, as though he has died multiple times. As the Hunters progress through the vault, the caskets become larger, and the statues more monstrous and draconic. Opening any of the graves reveals a skeleton roughly analogous to the statue on top and a smattering of mouldered grave goods.

At the far end, the monstrous porcine form of The Vicar (page 42) squats in front of an enormous rusted bank vault door surrounded by ranks of the singing faithful. It’s distracted for now, but it’s in your way.

6. The Root Hole

Root: As the Bishop grew in power and influence, he retreated from the public eye and the responsibilities associated with his station. When he finally Hollowed, he had not been seen by anyone other than his closest attendants for months.

A winding cave system that stretches down far beneath the Priory and deep into the earth. Though parts of it are open enough to stand up straight without banging your head, the majority of the system is made up of cramped tunnels, claustrophobic burrows and partially-flooded passageways. Thankfully, the route down isn’t hard to determine: torn fabric, scuff marks and cracked outcroppings mark frequent passage.

While the walls start as dirt and mangled masonry, they quickly transition to tightly-packed gold and gems. Cut rubies and jagged emeralds jut out of tunnel walls, a loose scree of bent coins lies precariously underfoot, and tarnished jewellery chain seems to grasp and snare the Hunters as they descend. Near the end of the crawl, a luminescent root shines from the depths of a foot-tall passageway – a Relic.

Roots of All Evil

Jagged cut diamonds and twisted coins tear at knees and hands.

Quick or Sharp TN11, 4/1. Bypass the Hazard to get the Rowan Roots.

Rowan Roots

Gold veined roots with an opalescent sheen; the rowan tree is holy, and a ward against malevolent forces.

7. The Golden Pit

Root: The Bishop was happy to throw away his faith, his duty and his life in search of greater wealth.

A network of tunnels dug by claws and loaded with stolen wealth. The floor is lousy with discarded crowns, bent sceptres, dented chalices and torn-up portraits. Huge piles of coins stretch to every wall. Scant illumination is provided by flickering torches mounted in alcoves along the sides of the chamber. Murals, lovingly painted by long-dead hands, depict the Bishop receiving tithes from his diocese – crowds of nobles and peasants alike arrive to shovel money directly into his great distended mouth.

In the shifting darkness at the centre The Bishop hangs motionless in the shadows, withered arms by his side, gut swollen with gold. As the Hunters approach, they notice an enormous eye open on each side of him, and the truth is revealed: his desiccated remains are protruding from the forehead of the great dragon that he has become in his greed.

The Guardian Hound

The Hound is a simple beast: a physical manifestation of the Bishop’s frantic desire to protect what he believes to be his. It takes the form of a grotesquely-muscled dog – half-pitbull, half-wolfhound – and stands taller than a man at the shoulder. A rusted chain hangs from its collar, binding it to a post in the centre of its graveyard hunting ground.

Any fur it once possessed has been shaved away by terrified attendants and brought to the Tailor to weave into clothing; the creature’s bare skin is covered in Viridian holy text instead.

Tactics

The Guardian Hound is intended to teach players the basic mechanics of the game whilst gently threatening them with evisceration. Your primary attack is Grab and Shake – you get to hit one Hunter with another Hunter, which is nice – and your manoeuvre, Bounding Leap, sets it up beautifully. You get to place 4 Threat at the end of your turn, so make sure to use it to trigger Snap and Snarl as often as possible or enhance Grab and Shake. If the Hunters are clever and try to get behind you, Wild Chain lets you attack all close areas aside from Front – and the Turn Aroundstandard manoeuvre lets you make sure more people are in the target areas.

Tactics

The Tailor relies on two important mechanics: Curse and Threat. Puncture, one of your Interrupts, lets you put Curse on a Hunter – as does A Thousand Needles, one of your attacks – and every time a Hunter attacks, they take Resolve damage equal to their Curse. (Usually, players ignore it until it’s too late.) Try to spread out your Curse rather than focusing on one target; using Crimson Threads,it’s not difficult to remove it from one Hunter, but removing it from everyone is very tricky. You place one additional Threat on a Hunter’s area when they successfully attack you, and you can move Threat around with Vicious Web (or use it to move Hunters with Bound For Death). Use those to get Threat in place to spend to enhance your Ravenous Mangle attack. If you can catch a Hunter’s arm in your gears, consider the fight won.

The Vicar

The domestic pig is sacred to the Viridians; it is the ultimate expression of mankind’s dominance over nature. The Vicar was once human – a priest who collaborated with the Bishop, covering up his many indiscretions and taking a sizeable cut for himself. Drawn into the Hollow, he has transformed into a bloated pig-creature, and his vestments have long ago torn and mouldered into filthy rags.

Surrounding him is a gang of malnourished lay preachers and attendants bearing improvised polearms and battered shields. On occasion, in moments of desperate hunger, he will grab one of them and eat them whole. Plenty more where they came from.

The Bishop

A despicable wyrm the size of a coal barge, scales rimmed with gold, eyes gleaming like rubies. Jackets, robes and tapestries have been buttoned and lashed round the creature’s massive bulk, and each of its clawed feet is dripping with rings, bangles and inlaid porcelain. Growing out of, or perhaps attached to, the wyrm’s noseless face is the Bishop’s limp body – all that remains of the man’s original form. It sits defiant on the dragon’s brow, taunting the Hunters from a safe distance.

Specials

Avaricious Aura. Your hands shake and your stomach twists with a sudden and desperate hunger. At the start of the Bishop’s turn, all Hunters in Close suffer 1 Resolve damage.

Gilded Scales. The benefits of wealth. Reduce all instances of Resolve damage suffered by the Entity by 1 (to a minimum of 1).

Interrupt

Money Sink. Tarnished coins and rotting finery make for unstable footing. Cost: 2 Threat. Target all Hunters in any area. Choose one:

  • Shift targets into an adjacent area.
  • Remove all Focus from targets.
  • Attack all targets. Strong TN9, 2/1.

Attacks

Liquid Assets. Try as it might, the wyrm can’t derive any nutrients from the coins it eats – but it can retch them up like grapeshot . Spend 3 Resolve. Target all Hunters in either Front or one Flank area, and all Hunters in the Ranged area adjacent to the initial target area. Quick TN9, 3/3.

Thrash. Frantic, panicked, a storm of teeth and claws. Target all Hunters in any two adjacent Close areas. Hard TN11, 2/3.

Devour. Gold is good, but nothing beats fresh meat.Target one Hunter in Front. Strong TN8, 3/4. If this attack reduces a Hunter to 0 Wounds, they are immediately killed (remove them from the grid) and the Bishop regains 6 Resolve.

Manoeuvres

Flee. Diving beneath a sea of coin and jewel to lick its wounds. Restore X Resolve, where X is 6 minus 2 for each Hunter at Close.

Sunk Cost. As the beast descends beneath the sea of gold, the terrain collapses. Make a Money Sink
Interrupt action, ignoring the usual Threat cost.

Tactics

The Bishop is a longer fight than the others thanks to the damage reduction provided by Gilded Scales. You’ll be spending a lot of Resolve to trigger Liquid Assets, so don’t be afraid to clear people out of Close with a Money Sink Interrupt between turns to make the most out of Flee .
(Or Scurry, if the Doom is high enough.) Something about targeting a Close and a Ranged area at the same time puts the fear of God into players – as does recovering 6 Resolve and taking a Hunter from standing to
dead on a successful Devour attack. Have fun with it.

When First Broken

By default, the Bishop doesn’t have an ability that triggers when he’s Broken for the first time. But the Doom track adds one, if the players haven’t made their way swiftly through his domain.

DOOM

8 Ceremonial Armor . Increase
Resolve to 9.

10 Overconfident. Replace the Flee manoeuvre with: Scurry. Restore X Resolve, where X is 6 minus 1 for each Hunter at Close.

13 Undermine. The Bishop gains the following ability: When First Broken: Undermine. Coins roar in the depths. Make a Money Sink attack targeting two adjacent areas. This attack does not cost Threat.

What's Next?

Following the Baptism with the Bishop’s ichor, the Hollow begins to fluctuate and collapse without the wickedness that once fuelled it.

Ask the Hunters how they leave the place – do they take a shortcut through the Sanctum? Does a writhing portal burst from the Bishop’s enormous corpse as golden sinkholes threaten to claim their lives? Is it an arduous climb back up through the Root Hole, and out to the surface, and from there an improvised ritual of their own to escape?

Whatever they choose, they leave and emerge in the Refuge – their own version of the Sanctum, an interdimensional coaching inn run by a grumbling landlord bound to the place known as the Keeper. A fire roars in the hearth and the Keeper squints at them as he pours a pint of beer.

“Been wondering when you lot would show up. Sit down. And close the door, will you? You’ll let all the bloody evil in.”

Handouts

The Priest

Play as the Priest if you want to:

  • Heal and support other Hunters
  • Blow holes in things with your Shotgun
  • Have a religious connection with the Hollow

Y ou are a member of the religious Viridian sect who sing of the glorious bounties that lay beneath the earth. Many of your order are keen horticulturalists and farmers, and you have taken it upon yourself to prune the rot from the Isles itself rather than limit yourself to a sanctuary garden.

Background: SWINEHERD. The pig is a sacred animal to Viridians, and you once roved the countryside at the head of a gang of these ravenous beasts. Roll with advantage during Exploration when you: interact with animals, get up to your elbows in mud and shit, persuade someone to let you into their nice clean home, etc.

The Soldier

Play as the Soldier if you want to:

  • Absorb lots of damage
  • Draw attention away from your allies
  • Protect and enhance other hunters

T he Crown maintains huge, inefficient and badly-led armies – and you were one of the thousands of soldiers that trudged into battle after battle at the behest of their betters. After your regiment was all but wiped out in a suicidal charge on foreign soil, you returned to the Isles with a broken gun in your hands and bitterness in your heart.

Background: Regimental Peon. Sworn into service before birth, you were marked as a militant by the brand on your brow. Roll with advantage during Exploration when you: withstand horrible conditions, march for hours, stand in the rain, etc.

The Agent

Play as the Agent if you want to:

  • Support other Hunters.
  • Manipulate the Entity’s attention.
  • Draw power from inflicting Wounds.

You used to collect unpaid taxes for the House – the (supposedly) democratic government of the Isles that sits awkwardly alongside the Crown. After an investigation led you into a Hollow, you realised that the corruption you’d witnessed was only the tip of the iceberg, and you had more important things to do with your life than kicking the tar out of someone for a few quid.

Background: Loss Administrator. You were a high-end debt collector, going after the sort of people who could afford to hire security and install iron vaults but not to pay their bloody taxes. Roll with advantage during Exploration when you: sneak in somewhere you shouldn’t be, intimidate and throw your weight around, deal with money, etc.

The Noble

Play as the Noble if you want to:

  • Deal lots of Wound damage.
  • Survive deadly attacks.
  • Be the anchor point for the group

Y ou are the scion of one of the lesser noble families cosying up to the King. The obligations associated with your position saw you do some terrible things at your elders’ command, and you spent your days wracked with guilt and grief. In the depths of your despair you glimpsed a great and terrible malignancy at the heart of the Isles. It is your duty to defend this land, and you have a soul full of sin to scrub clean.

Background: Blueblood. Your nobility gives you the edge in matters of etiquette and history. Roll with advantage when you: stand on ceremony, refer to the glorious if chequered past of the Isles, dress up, impress the middle classes, etc.

The Experiment

Play as the Experiment if you want to:

  • Inflict lots of damage.
  • Be fast and fragile.
  • Draw strength from the Entity’s pain.

T he Conclave is a secretive order of scientists, doctors and occultists that plumb the depths of Hollows and the strange energies found inside. Destitute and desperate, you volunteered for experimentation and went under their knives several years ago. Since then you’ve been haunted by visions of unreal worlds and monsters beyond reckoning – but you can find peace by delving headlong into the nightmare and bleeding it dry.

Background: Test Subject. After the experiments concluded, you were left with a network of scars, resonant metals implanted under your skin, and an uncanny sense of the malignancies that gnaw ravenous at the Isles. Roll with advantage during Exploration when you: use your unnatural senses, withstand punishment or torture, escape a dire situation, etc.
