Exploration

When you’re in a Hollow but you’re not in a fight against an Entity, you’re in the Exploration stage of the game.

Surrounding each Entity in a Hollow are blasted hinterlands of rotten unreality where people, places and things have been absorbed from the real world and remade into unsettling new forms. Navigating these areas isn’t as dangerous as going-to-toe with giant monsters, but it has the potential to cause tremendous harm or misfortune – or, if you’re clever about it, give you an edge to defeating your enemies.

Exploration in Hollows is more about creating a story together than it is about careful mechanical play. GM, this is your opportunity to make things up, patch things together, change the pacing and play with emotion, outside of the technical crunch of Hollows combat.

Rolling During Exploration

You only need to roll dice when you’re interacting with a Hazard (an obstacle or danger) or a Thrall (a person or creature) – when something is at stake. No risk, no roll.

When you’re asked to make a roll, first decide what stat is applicable to the task you’re trying to achieve:

  • S trong : Destructive, athletic or vicious.
  • H ard : Tough, resistant, grim or intimidating.
  • Q uick : Fast, decisive, sneaky or deceitful.
  • S harp : Keen-eyed, aware and intelligent.
  • W ise : Intuitive, introspective or mystical.

Once you’ve chosen your stat, roll as described in the basic rules
(page 6). Remember: if you act in accordance with your background during Exploration, you may be able to roll with advantage. It’s up to you to decide how that should play out at the table, and up to the GM to decide how strict or permissive they want to be in allowing this.

There are no specific rules for characters helping one another; it's assumed that everyone is working as a team to overcome challenges.

On the other hand, if a Hunter is alone and unable to receive assistance from their teammates, the GM can increase the TN of any task they attempt by 2 to reflect their exposed state.

Hazards

Hazards are dangers which the Hunters must overcome, bypass or neutralise to gain access to a location or reward. Usually, only one Hunter from the group must bypass the Hazard in order to move forward. Hazards have three pieces of information associated with them:

  • Target Number: The TN required to avoid damage entirely.
  • Stat: A suggested stat that Hunters should test to overcome, bypass or neutralise the Hazard. It's up to the players and the GM which stat is used – if someone suggests a clever solution using something other than what's listed, go for it.
  • Damage: Listed as X/Y. The first number is Resolve damage, and the second is Wound damage; these function in the same way as Entity attack damage.

When you attempt to overcome a Hazard, resolve the roll as follows:

Hunt Checks

The bridge between exploration and Entity combat is a Hunt Check. When a party is in an Entity’s lair, they make a Hunt Check to corner the creature (or flush it out, draw it from a hiding place, convince it to approach them, and so on) using a suitable stat; usually there’s a Hazard for the group to overcome in order to reach the Entity. The worse they roll, the more of a chance the Entity has to prepare for their approach, represented by increased Doom.

Improvising Problems

GM: sometimes a player will do something that requires you to come up with a challenge off the top of your head. In this case, feel free to adapt the Hazards and Thralls listed in the Grisham Priory Hollow and create your own (or just use them with the rules as written, and change the descriptions). Don’t worry about balancing out damage or Doom to be “fair” – the Hunters can take it.

Thralls

Thralls are denizens of the Hollow that oppose and obstruct you, intentionally or otherwise: creatures that must be fought, bargained with or evaded. Not every non-player character in a Hollow is a Thrall – only those that act against the Hunters – so the GM can populate the Hollow with as many characters as they please.

Thralls have four pieces of information associated with them:

  • Target Number: The TN used on all rolls involving the Thrall.
  • Stat: A suggested stat that Hunters should test to overcome the Thrall. Players can choose different ways to tackle the Thrall; while most Thralls in this quickstart suggest how to damage or fight them, there are always non-violent options.
  • Damage: Listed as X/Y. These function in the same way as Entity attack damage: the first number is the Resolve damage the Thrall inflicts with its attacks, and the second is the Wound damage.
  • Health: X/Y. The first number is Thrall Resolve and the second is Wounds.

When you interact with a Thrall with the intent to harm it, roll as though you were attacking an Entity using one of your core Weapon attacks (page 16). Compare the result of your roll to the Target Number of the Thrall and to the stat required, according to the Attack column below. Once you’ve resolved your action, the Thrall has a chance to retaliate. It makes an immediate attack against one of your stats with the damage listed in its profile. Non-Violent Solutions

You don’t have to kill everything you meet: you can usually talk, sneak, think or lie your way out of a problem during Exploration. If what you want to do is risky or time-consuming but won’t directly harm the Thrall, treat it as a Hazard with the same TN and Damage but no Health, and roll as normal.

Dying During Exploration

It’s possible, but unlikely, that a Hunter will be reduced to 0 Wounds during the Exploration phase. In this case, they awaken in the Sanctum or the Refuge as normal (see page <?>).

Rewards

Successful exploration can result in one (or more) of these rewards: greater understanding of the Hollow and its Lord, access to an adjacent zone, or Rumours and Relics. Rumours are information about Entities that give the players mechanical advantage in battle. Relics are important items resonant with power: they unlock combat manoeuvres or advantages against certain enemies.

Doom

The Hollow doesn’t stand still and wait for the Hunters to cleanse it. The longer they take, and the more disruption they cause, the more it will act against them. This hostility is represented by Doom.

Doom is shared between all Hunters and tracked by the GM. The following events increase Doom:

Hunter rolls a 20 (critical failure) during Exploration

+1 Doom

Hunter chooses to re-roll a failed check during Exploration

+1 Doom

Hunters perform a Baptism (page XX)

+1 Doom

Hunter fails an action with Doom attached as a stake

+1/+2/+3 Doom (GM’s choice)

Hunter opens the door to the Sanctum

+3 Doom

The following events reduce Doom:

Hunter rolls a critical success during Exploration

-1 Doom

Before a battle against an Entity begins, the GM should check the Entity’s Doom track and compare it to the current Doom for the Hollow. Each result with a value equal to or lower than the current Doom takes effect.

Player Tips

Describe your actions. What does it look like when your character does things? What do their Weapons look like? How do they react to being hurt? Work with the GM to paint the world of Hollows.
Read the rules. Make sure you know what your Weapon abilities do and how they interact with other players. This game is a lot more fun when you understand how all the pieces fit together and you can use them effectively.
Lean in to your role . Hollows is designed so that not everyone can do everything, but there are many different combinations of Weapons and powers that give the game a different flavour. If you’re set up as a tank whose abilities all trigger when you’re the centre of attention, then aim to do that as much as you can, and have fun with it. In the core game, you can always change your setup later on.
Take risks. It’s only a game. And even if your character dies, they’ll get right back up again and start causing havoc. Do the exciting thing, trust your luck, and have a good time.

Gamesmaster Tips

Describe the world. Hollows are wretched places and Entities are grisly nightmares, and it’s up to you to paint the world as evocatively as possible. Mention what the Hunters can see, smell, hear, touch or taste in any given moment. If you can, stand up and stomp around to show the thunderous footsteps of a behemoth, cower and shiver to portray a terrified ghoul, and always remember that the place itself is hostile and wants the Hunters to die.
When a player rolls dice, tell them what happens. Whenever
a player makes an action in a game, more than anything they want something to happen – good or bad isn’t really important. They want to feel as though their actions have consequences, so give them some.
Balance story with fighting. While combat in Hollows is tightly mechanical and detailed, exploration is meant to allow for much more free-form play. The game should flow back and forth between periods of roleplay, exploration and discovery, followed by massive, epic boss fights where everyone needs to pay close attention to the rules. Spacing these out and managing them is your job as GM, and it’s important to pace things so that your group finds a good balance that works for them.
Go hard . Hollows is intended to be vicious, and the mechanics mean that player characters can die, get back up, and come back for another go with better information. Be brutal and uncompromising; choose the worst thing for the player characters. Punish mistakes. If you pull your punches with Entities and your players understand their abilities, the game can be too easy. Don’t give the players an inch in combat.
